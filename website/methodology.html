Arboris

Methodology

How Arboris answers, and how the energy numbers are worked out. Everything here is an estimate from provider-reported tokens, never a physical data-centre measurement.

How Arboris works

Every message runs through three steps before it is answered.

1. PromptShrink

Removes filler words from your question before it reaches the model.

2. Session cache

Repeats a previous answer when you ask the same thing twice in a session. No model call, no new tokens.

3. Right-sized answers

Picks the shortest sufficient answer length by question length: Instant (12 words or fewer, ~2 sentences, 120 max tokens), Balanced (13–34 words, ~4 sentences, 240 max tokens), or Thorough (35+ words, a complexity phrase, or 2+ questions, ~6 sentences, 400 max tokens). Need a fuller explanation? Click More detail under any live answer to request an expanded Thorough response.

How the numbers work

Generating tokens uses far more compute than reading prompt tokens.

  • Energy: output × 0.0006 Wh + input × 0.0006 / 800 Wh (Epoch AI 2025; Ruf & Detyniecki HotCarbon ’26)
  • Carbon: Wh / 1000 × 400 gCO₂e/kWh (0.00024 g per output token)
  • Cooling water: Wh × 0.1–0.3 mL (closed-loop data-centre WUE; generation water excluded)

The "typical chatbot" baseline is the same reply at a modeled verbose length: about 2.4× the output tokens for an unconstrained answer (2.6× for Canopy, 1.9× for Forest/Full, 3.4× for Sprout), or a measured baseline when you enable "Measure baseline" in settings. A local or cached answer has a 45-token baseline. When a reply saves less than 10%, the receipt says "about the same as a typical chatbot" instead of a percent.

The receipt

Under each answer, the bar is that reply's share of a typical chatbot answer: the grey track is the full length of a typical answer, the teal fill is how far this reply went, and the empty part is the saving. Under the bar, one line puts the whole chat's saving so far into everyday terms, rotating by message: a phone charge (10W charger, 1 Wh is about 6 minutes), streaming video on a phone (~3W), a phone battery (~15 Wh for a full charge), Google searches (~0.3 Wh each), or reading on an e-reader (~1W). Phone-charge equivalent assumes a 10W charger (1 Wh is about 6 minutes of charging).

Finland reference & limits

Clean-energy reference: Google Cloud Finland (europe-north1): ~98% carbon-free energy, 30 gCO₂eq/kWh.

Trees

Arboris plants one tree for every 10,000 messages. The trees are planted by hand, from seed, by Grant, at his local barn.

Trees planted so far: 20 (100 seeds planted at his local barn; about 1 in 5 become a young tree. Updated by hand as trees actually establish).

Why 10,000? A typical Arboris answer is about 80 output tokens, about 0.02 g of CO2e. A mature tree absorbs about 21 kg of CO2 a year, about 800 kg over a 40 year life. So one tree for one year offsets about 1 million Arboris queries, and one tree over its life offsets about 40 million. At 1 tree per 10,000 messages, the planting is about 100 times the carbon the messages emit, so the offset holds even with conservative numbers.

Honest notes. Seeds are not trees. Only about 1 in 5 seeds becomes a young tree, so about 5 seeds are planted for each tree counted, and only trees that actually establish are counted. A seed also takes a decade or more to absorb carbon at the full rate, so the planting offsets the carbon over the coming decades, not today. The count above is trees planted, not carbon offset today.

How this compares to EcoGPT. EcoGPT plants 1 tree per 100 chats free (1 per 50 on Pro) through Trees for the Future. That is 100 times more trees per message than Arboris, about 10,000 times the carbon per year versus about 100 times for Arboris. Both rates massively over-offset the actual carbon. The difference is that EcoGPT outsources the planting to a verified organisation and publishes no per-query energy figure, so you cannot check their offset math from their own numbers, while Arboris plants by hand, publishes the math above, and counts only trees that establish.
